## PITCH 1: SMYLO

### Business Snapshot and Vision

Smylo is India's first 100% natural fresh-meat cat food brand, launched in November 2024 as a research-led, cat-exclusive nutrition company. The Delhi-based startup tackles a fundamental market gap: while India's pet food industry explodes with 35% annual growth in pet adoption, the market overwhelmingly caters to dogs, leaving 70+ million cat parents dependent on imported products or generic dog food formulations inappropriately repackaged for felines.

Founded on a deeply personal tragedy—the loss of a beloved pet due to inadequate nutrition options—Smylo's founders Abhishek Agrawal and Kartikeya Gupta built a challenger brand with singular focus: addressing cats' obligate carnivore biology. Unlike dogs (omnivores), cats require high-protein, meat-forward diets. Yet most Indian cat food contains carb-heavy formulations with artificial preservatives, fillers, and meat by-products—nutritionally deficient and potentially harmful.

Smylo's vision extends beyond product to category creation. The founders position themselves not as pet food sellers but as advocates reshaping India's understanding of feline nutrition. Their vet-formulated recipes use real fresh meat (chicken, tuna, mackerel), zero artificial preservatives, colors, or flavors, and transparent ingredient lists. The subscription-first model creates recurring revenue while building a loyal community of informed cat parents.

The brand's confidence in product quality manifested dramatically on Shark Tank: co-founder Kartikeya ate the cat food on-air, reinforcing ingredient purity and safety—a standout viral moment demonstrating conviction in clean-label positioning.

### Founders Background

**Abhishek Agrawal** (Co-Founder) holds a B.Tech from IIT Delhi, one of India's most prestigious engineering institutions. Before entrepreneurship, he worked at **Titan Capital** (Kunal Bahl's investment firm), gaining intimate exposure to startup ecosystems, consumer brand building, and investor perspectives. This insider knowledge proved invaluable when pitching Kunal on Shark Tank—Abhishek had been a valued team member who transitioned from investor-side to founder journey.

**Kartikeya Gupta** (Co-Founder) earned his degree from BITS Pilani, another top-tier Indian engineering school. His background complements Abhishek's with technical and operational strengths. Together, they represent the classic IIT-BITS founder duo—technical excellence combined with business acumen.

Their entrepreneurial trigger was visceral: losing a pet and discovering India's cat food market was fundamentally broken. Existing options were either expensive imports (₹800-1200/kg) or nutritionally inadequate domestic products. This personal pain point transformed into market insight: cat parents desperately needed affordable, high-quality, India-made nutrition tailored to feline biology.

### Key Business Terms in Action

**Obligate Carnivore:** Central to Smylo's pitch, this biological term describes cats' physiological requirement for meat-based diets. Unlike omnivores (dogs, humans) who can digest carbs efficiently, cats lack enzymes to process plant proteins optimally. This scientific distinction became the foundation of Smylo's differentiation—most Indian cat foods are high-carb formulations borrowed from dog food recipes, ignoring feline biology. Educating Sharks on this term justified premium pricing and category-specific focus.

**Subscription Model:** Smylo operates subscription-first, where customers receive recurring monthly deliveries. This creates predictable revenue, higher Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), and improved inventory planning. Subscriptions also build behavioral lock-in—once cats adjust to Smylo, switching costs increase (cats resist diet changes). The model generates 40-50% higher margins than one-time purchases due to reduced CAC amortized across multiple orders.

**Clean Label / Transparency:** "Clean label" refers to minimal, recognizable ingredients without chemicals pet parents can't pronounce. Smylo's ingredient list—fresh chicken, water, natural vitamins—contrasts sharply with competitor lists full of preservatives, artificial flavors, and meat "by-products" (industry euphemism for low-quality protein sources). Kartikeya eating the cat food on-air demonstrated ultimate clean-label confidence—if it's safe for humans, it's certainly safe for cats.

**Quick Commerce Integration:** Smylo's presence on Blinkit, Zepto, and Swiggy Instamart enables 10-30 minute delivery—critical for impulse purchases and emergency stock-outs. Quick commerce channels command higher margins than traditional e-commerce due to convenience premium, and create trial opportunities (customers can order single pack to test without committing to subscription).

**Advisory Equity:** The 5% advisory stake given to Sharks is non-cash equity awarded in exchange for mentorship, network access, and strategic guidance. It dilutes founders but doesn't drain company cash—appropriate for early-stage startups needing expertise more than capital. It also aligns Sharks' incentives long-term—they benefit only if Smylo succeeds.

**Niche Market Focus:** Rather than spreading across dogs, birds, fish (like competitors), Smylo obsesses over one category: cats. This "1-inch wide, 1-mile deep" strategy builds category expertise, operational efficiency (simplified supply chain), and brand authority. Sharks invest in focus—founders trying to be everything to everyone usually achieve nothing for no one.

### Takeaways for Learners

**1. Personal Pain Points Create Authentic Businesses:** Smylo emerged from losing a pet—founders lived the customer problem viscerally. Investors detect authentic founder-market fit versus opportunistic market-chasing. Personal stories resonate emotionally and signal long-term commitment.

**2. Niche Focus Beats Broad Positioning:** "Cat-only" seemed limiting; founders turned it into strength. In crowded markets, 1-inch wide and 1-mile deep beats 1-mile wide and 1-inch deep. Specialization builds expertise, community, and defensibility competitors can't easily replicate.

**3. Viral Moments Drive Awareness:** Kartikeya eating cat food generated 5-6x follower growth and 30% CAC reduction. Creative, authentic moments create word-of-mouth impossible to buy with advertising. The ROI on that single act likely exceeded ₹10-20 lakh in equivalent marketing spend.

**4. Clean Label Requires Demonstration, Not Declaration:** Anyone can claim "natural" or "healthy." Smylo proved it by consuming the product. Actions trump claims. In health/wellness categories, demonstration destroys skepticism better than certifications or testimonials.

**5. Subscription Models Create Compounding Value:** 45% repeat rate means every customer acquired compounds—first order covers CAC, subsequent orders are pure margin. The math: if CAC is ₹500 and customer makes 4 purchases averaging ₹400 each, CLV is ₹1,600—a 3.2x return. Subscriptions make marginal economics exponentially better.

**6. Leveraging Existing Relationships:** Abhishek's Titan Capital background created pre-existing trust with Kunal. Rather than hide this, he leveraged it openly. Entrepreneurs should activate their networks strategically—investors who know you have less due diligence risk.

**7. Advisory Equity Attracts Better Partners:** Offering 5% non-cash equity signaled Smylo valued guidance over just money. This attracted Sharks seeking long-term relationships versus quick flips. Founders should think beyond capital—who brings networks, expertise, credibility that accelerates growth?

**8. Quick Commerce is New Retail:** Smylo's integration with Blinkit/Zepto demonstrates evolved distribution. Quick commerce offers better economics than traditional e-commerce (no shipping costs) and retail (no distributor margins) while providing customer convenience. D2C brands ignoring quick commerce miss major growth channel.

**9. Education as Market Creation:** Smylo didn't just sell cat food—they educated cat parents on obligate carnivore biology. Market creation requires consumer education. The founders positioned themselves as thought leaders, not just merchants, building trust that competitors can't easily erode.

**10. Post-Shark Tank Marketing Multiplier:** The visibility spike reduced CAC 30% and increased Tier II-III demand. Shark Tank isn't just about funding—it's marketing accelerator compressing years of brand building into single episode. Founders should prepare to capitalize on post-show traffic surge with infrastructure to handle demand.

## PITCH 2: NOOTIE

### Business Snapshot and Vision

Nootie is a 27-year-legacy pet care brand operating under parent company Pet Point India Pvt. Ltd., founded by the Mahendru family. What began as brick-and-mortar pet stores (The Pet Point) in 2003 evolved into Nootie—a contemporary mass-premium pet nutrition and grooming brand serving 70,000+ families across India.

The business spans dual engines: (1) **Nootie** brand products (dog food, biscuits, supplements, grooming items) using 100% human-grade ingredients, and (2) **The Pet Point** retail chain with 8 physical stores providing omnichannel touchpoints. This hybrid model leverages decades of trust built through retail while modernizing via D2C channels, quick commerce, and Amazon.

Nootie's vision transcends product sales toward holistic pet wellness. The brand emphasizes science-backed nutrition, veterinary partnerships, and accessibility—epitomized by their ₹10 dog biscuit pack on Swiggy Instamart designed to enable feeding stray/community dogs. This social consciousness differentiates Nootie from pure-profit competitors, resonating with Sharks focused on purpose-driven brands.

The founders—Anil (father), Akshay (son/CEO), and Karan (son)—represent intergenerational entrepreneurship where legacy meets innovation. Akshay's biotech engineering education combined with father's 20+ years domain expertise creates rare blend: modern business acumen grounded in category depth.

### Founders Background

**Anil Mahendru** (Father, Co-Founder) established The Pet Point in 2003, pioneering organized pet retail in India when the industry was nascent. Over two decades, he built relationships with thousands of pet parents, veterinarians, and suppliers—creating invaluable distribution networks and market understanding. His legacy provided Nootie with trust infrastructure competitors take years to build.

**Akshay Mahendru** (Son, Co-Founder & CEO) holds a Bachelor of Technology from Amity University with specialization in biotechnology. His technical education gave him scientific lens to approach pet nutrition, but his true calling came from lifelong exposure to animals via father. Akshay is also author of *"Raising Happy Dogs,"* a book sharing practical guidance for pet parents—establishing thought leadership beyond commerce.

Over 20 years in the industry, Akshay pioneered Nootie's expansion across Amazon, quick commerce (Zepto, Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart), and retail partnerships. His LinkedIn profile reveals entrepreneurial journey milestones: founding Nootie to provide premium-quality products at affordable prices, building nationwide omnichannel presence, and scaling to serve lakhs of customers.

**Karan Mahendru** (Son, Co-Founder & Director) complements Akshay's vision with operational expertise, managing retail stores, logistics, and distributor relationships.

The three-generation family business creates continuity rare in startups. However, this legacy also created pressure during pitch—Akshay struggled with Sharks' questioning, becoming emotional as he tried to honor family's 20-year effort while demonstrating he could scale it further.

### Key Business Terms in Action

**Royalty Structure:** The 1% royalty until ₹1 crore recovered acts as downside protection for Sharks. Standard equity deals tie returns to exit (IPO/acquisition), but royalties provide cash flow during interim. For Nootie generating ₹24Cr annually, 1% royalty = ₹24L/year, meaning Sharks recoup ₹1Cr investment in ~4 years even if no exit occurs. This structure is investor-friendly for businesses with steady revenue but uncertain exit timelines.

**Mass-Premium Positioning:** "Mass-premium" refers to products priced between value (₹50-100) and luxury (₹500+) segments—₹150-250 range accessible to middle class yet signaling quality. Nootie's ₹179 Nibblr and ₹10 biscuits bookend this spectrum, capturing both premium aspirations and mass accessibility. This positioning requires operational excellence—delivering perceived premium quality at competitive costs.

**Omnichannel Distribution:** Nootie operates across online (Amazon, website), quick commerce (Zepto, Blinkit, Swiggy), and offline (8 stores, 100+ retail partners). Omnichannel isn't just multichannel—it's integrated experience where customers can discover online, buy in-store, or vice versa. The model maximizes reach but requires complex inventory management and consistent branding across touchpoints.

**Human-Grade Ingredients:** This term indicates ingredients meeting safety standards for human consumption, not just pet feed regulations. Akshay demonstrated this by eating a dog biscuit on camera (paralleling Smylo's cat food moment). "Human-grade" commands premium pricing as it assuages pet parent fears about quality, positioning Nootie above value brands using feed-grade inputs.

**Community Dogs (vs. Stray Dogs):** Language shift from "stray" to "community" dogs reflects compassionate positioning. Strays imply unwanted pests; community dogs are neighborhood fixtures deserving care. Nootie's ₹10 biscuit enables people to feed these animals affordably—corporate social responsibility (CSR) embedded in business model. This resonated with Namita who called it "thoughtful and noble."

**Legacy Business vs. Startup:** Nootie's 27-year history creates unique dynamics. Established businesses have customer trust, distribution, and cashflow startups lack—but also face legacy costs, slower innovation, and family dynamics. Sharks must evaluate if legacy is asset (moat) or liability (inertia). The 70% valuation haircut suggests Sharks saw both opportunity and execution risk.

### Takeaways for Learners

**1. Vulnerability Can Be Strength:** Akshay's "mama's boy" moment could've been career-ending on reality TV. Instead, it humanized him, creating empathy. Founders should be authentic—investors increasingly value genuine humans over polished pitchbots. Vulnerability, when honest not manipulative, builds connection.

**2. Know Your Numbers or Else:** Akshay's struggles answering basic financials became episode narrative. This isn't optional—founders MUST know CAC, LTV, margins, unit economics instantly. Investors interpret hesitation as either ignorance or dishonesty, both disqualifying.

**3. Legacy is Double-Edged:** 27 years brought trust and customers but also raised questions about why growth wasn't faster. Legacy businesses must demonstrate they're not stagnant—showing innovation, category expansion, or channel transformation that proves they can scale, not just maintain.

**4. Emotional Moments Don't Replace Fundamentals:** While Akshay's emotion created viral moment, it didn't save valuation—Sharks still cut it 70%. Investors feel empathy but write checks based on business fundamentals. Emotion opens doors; metrics close deals.

**5. Royalty Structures Signal Investor Confidence Levels:** Equity-only deals indicate high confidence in exits. Royalty + equity suggests investors want cash flow protection alongside upside participation. Founders should read deal structure subtext—royalties mean "we believe in stability, less in explosive growth."

**6. Social Mission Can Differentiate:** Nootie's ₹10 biscuits for community dogs created memorable positioning in crowded dog food market. Purpose-driven elements (when authentic, not marketing) resonate with consumers and investors increasingly prioritizing impact beyond profit.

**7. Omnichannel Requires Excellence:** Operating retail + online + quick commerce is complex—inventory across channels, consistent pricing, unified customer experience. Nootie's 8 stores could be asset (customer touchpoints) or liability (fixed costs limiting margins). Founders must prove omnichannel complexity is managed, not overwhelming them.

**8. Human-Grade Ingredients Require Proof:** Akshay eating dog biscuit demonstrated conviction Smylo paralleled with cat food. In health/wellness, claims need demonstration. Founders selling consumables should consider on-air consumption if product quality permits—visceral proof trumps certifications.

**9. Family Business Succession Challenges:** Akshay navigating father's presence showed generational transition pressures. Sons/daughters taking over family businesses must establish independent credibility while respecting legacy—walking tightrope between "I built on father's foundation" and "I'm not just riding coattails."

**10. Post-Pitch Narrative Matters:** The episode framed Akshay as underdog who overcame emotional moment to secure funding—positive spin versus "founder couldn't answer questions." Founders should recognize Shark Tank is entertainment; producers craft narratives. Emotional authenticity can generate favorable editing.

**11. Market Size ≠ Market Opportunity:** Dog food market is 7.5x larger than cat food, yet Smylo's focus won investor excitement equal to Nootie's reach. Bigger market with more competition isn't automatically better than smaller market with dominant positioning. Founders should question "TAM size" obsession—market share in niche beats market sliver in ocean.
