## PITCH 1: UPREAR BUILD

### Business Snapshot

Uprear Build is a Surat-based modular construction technology company manufacturing premium-grade prefabricated pods and infrastructure using advanced engineering, sustainable materials, and rapid deployment systems. Founded by **Shrikant Shah** (Purdue University-educated engineer), the company transforms traditional slow, expensive, quality-inconsistent construction into factory-built, quality-controlled, installation-ready modules delivered in weeks instead of months.

The flagship product: **Anganwadi Centre Modules** — fully equipped childcare facilities (classroom, kitchen, toilets, storage) that meet government specifications, arrive on-site pre-assembled, and become operational within days. These modules currently generate **70% of total revenue**, serving state governments' massive infrastructure rollouts under ICDS (Integrated Child Development Services) schemes.

Beyond Anganwadi, Uprear Build's portfolio includes:
- **Mobile Toilet Units:** Prefabricated sanitation blocks for events, construction sites, rural areas
- **Security Cabins:** Modular guard posts for industrial complexes, residential societies
- **Commercial Spaces:** Pop-up retail kiosks, food courts, office modules
- **Disaster Relief Shelters:** Rapidly deployable housing for emergency response

The business model operates B2G (Business-to-Government) for Anganwadi contracts and B2B (Business-to-Business) for commercial clients. The company owns manufacturing facilities, controls quality end-to-end, and maintains design IP around structural engineering optimized for Indian climate, transport logistics, and regulatory requirements.

### Business Vocabulary/Glossary

**Modular/Prefabricated Construction:** Buildings manufactured in factory-controlled environments as modules/sections, then transported and assembled on-site. Advantages: speed (60-80% faster), quality control (factory precision vs. weather-dependent on-site work), cost predictability (fixed pricing vs. cost overruns), sustainability (reduced waste, recyclable materials). India's modular construction market projected to grow 25% CAGR reaching ₹50,000+ crore by 2030.

**B2G (Business-to-Government):** Selling products/services directly to government entities via tenders, quotas, or contracts. Characteristics: large order sizes, payment delays (60-90 day terms typical), bureaucratic processes, stable demand tied to budget allocations. Uprear's 70% B2G concentration provides revenue scale but exposes to policy risk.

**Revenue Concentration Risk:** When significant portion of revenue comes from single client/segment. Investors discount valuations for concentrated businesses because: (1) Client loss = revenue collapse, (2) Pricing pressure (single buyer can squeeze margins), (3) Reduced negotiating leverage. Diversification across 5-10 major clients/segments mitigates this risk.

**Capital-Intensive Business:** Requires substantial upfront investment in fixed assets (factories, machinery, inventory) before generating revenue. Modular construction needs: manufacturing facilities (₹2-5 crore setup), raw materials inventory (steel, panels), transportation fleet, working capital for pre-production expenses. Capital intensity limits margins but creates barriers to entry (competitors need similar capital).

**EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, Amortization):** Operating profitability metric excluding financing costs and non-cash expenses. For manufacturing, EBITDA margins of 15-25% are healthy. Uprear's capital-light operations (potentially outsourced manufacturing or leased facilities) could achieve higher margins than traditional construction companies.

**Debt vs. Equity Financing:** 

**Debt Advantages (for Founder):**
- **Less dilutive:** Kunal's ₹1 crore debt required 0% equity vs. ₹1 crore equity requiring 2%
- **Tax-deductible:** Interest payments reduce taxable income
- **Fixed obligation:** Once repaid, no ongoing claims on profits/ownership

**Debt Disadvantages:**
- **Mandatory repayment:** Must pay ₹43L annually regardless of profitability
- **Cash flow strain:** Reduces reinvestment capital during growth phase
- **Failure risk:** Default can trigger liquidation/asset seizure

**Equity+Debt Hybrid** balances founder dilution control with investor downside protection — optimal for businesses with strong cash flows but uncertain exit timelines.

## PITCH 2: PAPER PRO

### Business Snapshot

Paper Pro is a patented milk adulteration detection kit enabling instant on-site testing for common adulterants (water, starch, urea, detergent, hydrogen peroxide, neutralizers) using simple chemical test cards. Founded by **Dhruv Tomar** after reading newspaper articles on widespread milk contamination, the Noida-based startup aims to empower consumers, dairy farmers, and food safety regulators with accessible, affordable quality control tools.

The product: **Test card strips** that change color when dipped in milk, indicating presence/absence of specific adulterants within seconds. No lab equipment required — anyone can test milk quality at point of purchase (vendor, home) before consumption.

The mission: Combat India's epidemic of milk adulteration affecting 70%+ of samples in many states (per FSSAI reports), causing health risks (kidney damage, stomach issues, toxicity) especially to children and pregnant women consuming adulterated milk daily.

**Expansion Plan:** Beyond milk, Paper Pro developing test kits for **paneer (cottage cheese)**, edible oils, honey — any food category prone to adulteration. The brand positioning: "Paper Pro" (testing solutions) not "Milk Pro" (milk-only focus) signals category ambition.

### Founder & Co-Founder Dynamics

**Dhruv Tomar** (Primary Founder, Operational Leader) — Drives day-to-day business, product development, customer acquisition, and fundraising. His origin story: Reading about milk adulteration sparked personal mission to solve problem scientifically.

**Rajat Jain** (Co-Founder, Part-Time/Passive) — Early-stage contributor who invested ₹3 lakh at ideation phase (high-risk moment) but remains **part-time** engaged due to other professional commitments. This passive involvement became central to Sharks' concerns.

**The Cap Table Problem:** At ideation stage, Dhruv gave Rajat **45% equity** for ₹3 lakh investment + initial contributions. Fast-forward to pitch: Paper Pro now revenue-generating, product-proven, but Rajat still holds 45% despite minimal ongoing involvement. This created stark imbalance flagged by Sharks.

**Core Product: Milk Adulteration Test Kit**

**Components:**
- 6-8 different test strips targeting specific adulterants:

### The Cap Table Crisis: What Went Wrong

**Current Equity Structure (Estimated):**

| Stakeholder | Equity % | Contribution | Ongoing Involvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dhruv Tomar** | ~50% | Founder, full-time operator, drives business | 100% active |
| **Rajat Jain** | 45% | ₹3 lakh investment at ideation, early support | Part-time/passive |
| **ESOP Pool** | 5% | Reserved for future hires | Unallocated |

**The Fatal Flaws:**

**Flaw 1 — Ideation-Stage Over-Dilution**

Giving away 45% equity for ₹3 lakh at idea stage is catastrophic for future fundraising. Here's why:

**Typical Early-Stage Equity Benchmarks:**
- **Ideation/pre-revenue:** Angel investors get 10-20% for ₹10-50 lakh
- **Seed stage (early revenue):** VCs get 15-25% for ₹50L-2Cr
- **Series A (proven model):** VCs get 20-30% for ₹5-15Cr

Paper Pro gave 45% for ₹3 lakh — implying ₹6.67 lakh valuation (₹3L / 45% = ₹6.67L). This is **absurdly low** even for pure idea stage.

**The Compounding Problem:**

Now seeking ₹1 crore for 5% (₹20Cr valuation). If successful:
- Dhruv: 50% × (1 - 0.05) = **47.5% remaining**
- Rajat: 45% × (1 - 0.05) = **42.75% remaining**
- New investor: **5%**
- ESOP: 5% × (1 - 0.05) = **4.75%**

After this round, **passive co-founder Rajat still controls 42.75% while active founder Dhruv has 47.5%** — nearly equal despite vastly different contributions.

Next fundraise (hypothetical ₹5 crore for 15%):
- Dhruv: 47.5% × (1 - 0.15) = **40.4%**
- Rajat: 42.75% × (1 - 0.15) = **36.3%**

Within two funding rounds, Dhruv (full-time founder) controls only 40.4% while Rajat (part-timer who invested ₹3L) holds 36.3%. This is **catastrophically imbalanced**.

**Flaw 2 — Insufficient ESOP Pool**

Only 5% reserved for employee stock options is **woefully inadequate** for scaling startup. Industry standards:

- **Early-stage startups:** 10-15% ESOP pool
- **Growth-stage:** 15-20% ESOP pool
- **Pre-IPO:** 20-25% ESOP pool

Why it matters: To attract senior talent (VP of Sales, CTO, CFO), Paper Pro needs to offer meaningful equity (1-3% each). With only 5% total pool, can attract maximum 2-3 key hires before depleting ESOP. Competitors with 15-20% pools will poach talent offering superior equity packages.

**Flaw 3 — Misaligned Risk-Reward**

Rajat invested ₹3 lakh when Paper Pro was just an idea (high risk), justifying equity compensation. But:
- **Then (ideation):** 100% failure risk, no product, no revenue, pure concept
- **Now (post-patent, commercialized):** Substantially de-risked, product exists, customers buying, patents granted

Rajat's 45% made sense at ideation risk level. Retaining 45% now — when risk profile transformed — creates value extraction from Dhruv (who bore execution burden).

**Shark's Reactions:**

**Anupam Mittal's Intervention:**  
Anupam **offered to buy out Rajat's stake**, recognizing the structure was untenable for future growth. This extraordinary move signaled:
1. Product has merit (Anupam willing to inject capital)
2. Cap structure must be fixed before additional investment
3. Passive co-founders holding disproportionate equity is deal-killer

**Other Sharks' Concerns:**
- **Control:** If Rajat passive but holds 42-45%, can he block decisions requiring super-majority?

### Cap Table Mistakes: Lessons for All Founders

**MISTAKE 1: Giving Large Equity at Ideation Stage**

**The Error:**  
45% for ₹3 lakh when company is just an idea reflects:
- Overvaluing capital (cash is precious but not irreplaceable)
- Undervaluing founder sweat equity (years of execution = far more valuable than early cash)
- Desperation (accepting any terms to get started)

**The Fix:**
- **Ideation-stage investors:** 10-15% for ₹5-10 lakh at most
- **Use convertible notes or SAFEs:** Delay valuation until traction proves company worth
- **Vesting schedules:** Even co-founders should vest over 3-4 years — if someone leaves early, equity reverts
- **Anti-dilution protections:** For founders, not early investors (maintain motivation through dilution)

**MISTAKE 2: Not Planning for Future Rounds**

**The Error:**  
Paper Pro gave 45% without modeling next 3-5 funding rounds. Every founder should cap table model:

| Round | Capital | Equity Given | Founder % Remaining |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ideation | ₹3L | 10% | 90% |
| Seed | ₹50L | 15% | 76.5% |
| Series A | ₹5Cr | 20% | 61.2% |
| Series B | ₹25Cr | 25% | 45.9% |

Even aggressive dilution leaves founder controlling 45%+ through Series B. Paper Pro's Dhruv will drop below 35% by Series A — loss of control, demotivation, potential board removal.

**The Fix:**
- **Reverse-engineer cap table:** Start with desired ownership at exit (40-50%), work backward to calculate how much to give each round
- **Milestone-based dilution:** Give equity in tranches tied to achievements, not upfront
- **Protect founder stake:** Use super-voting shares, board seat guarantees to maintain control despite dilution

**MISTAKE 3: Inadequate ESOP Pool**

**The Error:**  
5% ESOP cannot attract world-class talent required to build ₹100+ crore company.

**The Fix:**
- **Set aside 15-20% ESOP pool** at founding
- **Refresh ESOP** every funding round (ask new investors to dilute for ESOP expansion, not founder)
- **Use vesting cliffs:** 1-year cliff ensures employees stay committed before earning equity

**MISTAKE 4: Treating Part-Time Co-Founders Like Full-Time**

**The Error:**  
Rajat's part-time involvement doesn't justify equal equity to Dhruv's full-time commitment.

**The Fix:**
- **Full-time = 40-50% founder equity**, Part-time = 5-10% advisor equity
- **Vesting tied to involvement:** If Rajat reduces to 20% time, equity vests proportionally slower
- **Buyback clauses:** If co-founder goes part-time, company has right to buy back portion of equity at fair value

**MISTAKE 5: No Founder-Investor Alignment Documentation**

**The Error:**  
Verbal agreements ("we'll fix it later") don't protect anyone. Lack of formal shareholders' agreement creates disputes.

**The Fix:**
- **Shareholders' Agreement:** Document roles, responsibilities, equity, vesting, decision rights
- **Drag-along/tag-along rights:** Prevent minority blockers, ensure exit alignment
- **Voting agreements:** Specify decisions requiring unanimous vs. majority vs. board-only approval

### Business Vocabulary/Glossary

**Cap Table (Capitalization Table):** Spreadsheet showing who owns what percentage of the company and how ownership dilutes over funding rounds. Critical for founders to model future scenarios.

**Equity Dilution:** Reduction in ownership percentage when new shares issued. Example: Founder owns 100 shares (100% of 100 total). Company issues 25 new shares to investor. Founder still owns 100 shares but now represents 80% (100/125 total). Dilution = 20 percentage points.

**ESOP (Employee Stock Ownership Plan):** Pool of equity reserved for employee compensation. Startups use ESOPs to attract talent willing to accept lower salaries in exchange for equity upside.

**Vesting:** Equity granted over time, not upfront. Typical: 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff. Cliff = no equity until 12 months employment; then 25% vests. Remaining 75% vests monthly over next 3 years. If employee leaves before cliff, gets zero equity.

**Convertible Note:** Debt instrument converting to equity at future funding round. Allows early investors to provide capital before valuation determined. Example: Invest ₹10L convertible note. When Series A raises at ₹5Cr valuation, note converts to equity at 20% discount (₹4Cr effective valuation) rewarding early risk.

**SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity):** Like convertible note but not debt (no interest, no maturity). Converts to equity at next priced round. Increasingly popular for ideation/pre-seed funding.

**Super-Majority Rights:** Decisions requiring >50% approval (67% or 75% common thresholds). Used for major actions: raising capital, selling company, changing business model. Protects minority investors from majority steamrolling.

**Anti-Dilution Protection:** Mechanisms preventing early investors from losing value in down-rounds (lower valuation future raises). Full ratchet (most investor-friendly), weighted average (balanced) are common types.

**Drag-Along Rights:** Majority shareholders can force minority shareholders to join in sale of company. Prevents small holders blocking acquisitions.

**Tag-Along Rights:** If majority sells stake, minority has right to sell proportionally at same terms. Prevents majority extracting premium without sharing with minority.

### Business Vocabulary/Glossary

**Network-Aggregator Model (Asset-Light):** Platform connecting supply (ambulances) and demand (patients) without owning assets. Advantages: Low capex (no ambulance purchases), fast scaling (onboard partners vs. buy vehicles), flexible capacity (expand/contract based on demand). Disadvantages: Lower margins (commission vs. full revenue), less control (dependent on partner quality), churn risk (partners can leave platform).

**Golden Hour:** Medical principle that trauma/cardiac patients have 60-minute window for critical interventions determining survival likelihood. Every minute delayed reduces survival probability exponentially. Cosmo Health's 10-minute response targets maximizing golden hour utilization.

**Basic Life Support (BLS) vs. Advanced Life Support (ALS):** 

**BLS:** Paramedic-level care with CPR, oxygen, bandaging, vitals monitoring. Equipment cost: ₹2-5 lakh per ambulance.

**ALS:** Doctor/critical care nurse with advanced interventions (intubation, defibrillation, IV medications, cardiac monitoring). Equipment cost: ₹10-20 lakh per ambulance.

**Pricing differential:** ALS commands 2-3x higher rates than BLS due to skill level and equipment requirements.

**Utilization Rate:** Percentage of time assets (ambulances) are in revenue-generating use vs. idle. Typical Indian private ambulances: 30-40% utilization (idle 60-70% of time). Cosmo Health's value proposition to partners: increase utilization to 60-70% by aggregating demand, thus doubling partner revenue without buying more ambulances.

**Take Rate:** Commission percentage platform charges per transaction. Uber takes 20-25% of ride fare; food delivery platforms take 15-25%. Cosmo Health likely takes 15-30% of ambulance fare. Example: Patient pays ₹1,500 for BLS ambulance. Ambulance partner receives ₹1,050-1,275 (70-85%); Cosmo keeps ₹225-450 (15-30%). This take rate must cover: technology costs, customer acquisition, operations, and profit.

**Unit Economics:** Profitability per transaction. For Cosmo Health:

**Revenue per ride:** ₹300-500 (15-30% take rate on ₹1,500-2,000 average fare)

**Costs per ride:**
- **Tech/platform:** ₹20-30 (GPS, servers, app maintenance amortized)
- **Customer support:** ₹30-50 (call center, dispute resolution)
- **Payment processing:** ₹15-25 (credit card fees, UPI charges)
- **Marketing (blended CAC):** ₹100-200 per ride initially (decreases as brand builds)

**Gross profit per ride:** ₹50-250 (depending on take rate and customer acquisition efficiency)

**Contribution margin:** 10-40% early-stage; improves to 30-50% at scale as CAC amortizes across repeat users.

**Syndicate Investment:** Multiple investors co-funding a startup round. Benefits for founders: (1) Diversified expertise, (2) Larger network access, (3) Risk-sharing among investors increases total capital available. Risks: (1) Conflicting strategic advice, (2) Slower decision-making, (3) Coordination overhead (updating three investors vs. one).

**Regulatory Compliance (Healthcare):** Cosmo Health must navigate: (1) Ambulance licensing (state-level permits), (2) Paramedic certification requirements, (3) Medical equipment standards (BIS, ISO), (4) Data privacy (patient health information under DISHA), (5) Insurance/liability (accidents during transport), (6) Pricing regulations (some states cap ambulance fares).

### Takeaways

**1. Personal Pain Points Create Compelling Missions**  
Dr. Shivansh's father's heart attack wasn't just inspiration — it was validation. Lived experiences create authentic founder-market fit investors recognize.

**2. Network-Aggregator Models Scale Fast**  
Without owning ambulances (₹15-20 lakh each × 1,000 fleet = ₹150-200 crore capex), Cosmo can scale faster than asset-heavy competitors. Asset-light = capital-efficient growth.

**3. Three Sharks > One Shark (When Complementary)**  
Kunal's logistics + Anupam's consumer scaling + Kanika's healthcare = compounding strategic value. Founders should welcome syndicates when expertise is non-overlapping.

**4. Accept Dilution for Right Investors**  
9% vs. 4% (2.25x dilution) hurt short-term ownership but three Sharks accelerate growth potentially creating 10x larger outcome. Better to own 50% of ₹500 crore (₹250Cr) than 90% of ₹50 crore (₹45Cr).

**5. Healthcare Trust = Moat**  
Cosmo's real defensibility isn't technology (replicable) — it's trust (earned over thousands of positive emergency experiences). Trust compounds; competitors starting from zero face uphill battle.

**6. TAM Matters, But SOM Matters More**  
₹15,000 crore TAM sounds impressive, but realistic SOM (₹93 crore in 5 years) is what investors evaluate. Founders should be realistic about achievable market capture vs. total market size.

**7. Emergency Healthcare Demands Patient Capital**  
Building ambulance networks, regulatory compliance, and consumer trust takes years. Cosmo's investors must have 7-10 year horizons — not 3-year flip expectations. Healthcare is marathon, not sprint.
