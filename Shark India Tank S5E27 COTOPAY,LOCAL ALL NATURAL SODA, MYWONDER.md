# Shark Tank India — Season 5, Episode 27
## *Finance, Flavours and Future Minds*

**Sharks Present:** Aman Gupta · Anupam Mittal · Kanika Tekriwal · Kunal Bahl · Mohit Yadav
**Deals Closed:** 1 of 3 (CotoPay ✅ · Local All Natural Soda ❌ · MyWonder ❌)

> **What this episode really teaches:** Three architects of Aadhaar, CoWIN, and e-RUPI walked in and got called "underachievers" — the season's highest compliment. A husband-wife team who left America for Mizoram, rebuilt their company after regulatory setbacks, and then turned down the only deal they received because 25% was too much. And two second-time founders, one acquisition already behind them, who built a screen-free AI companion for children — and faced the question that Antler India's backing couldn't fully answer: can hardware compete with Alexa? Episode 27 is about the weight of credentials, the courage of conviction, and the precise cost of asking for more than the evidence supports.

---

## FOUNDER PROFILES — WHO BUILT THESE BUSINESSES AND WHAT IT TOOK

---

### COTOPAY — Aviral Gupta, Vidit Sidana & Uzair Syed Ahmed

**Aviral Gupta (Co-Founder & CEO)**
**Education:** Schooled in Dehradun → Master's in Management, **London Business School**
**Career:** **BharatPe** (Founding Team) → **National Health Authority** (CoWIN + e-RUPI) → CotoPay

Aviral is the only non-doctor in a family of doctors — a biographical detail that says everything about why he ended up at London Business School rather than a medical college, and why he joined BharatPe's founding team rather than a hospital. At BharatPe, he learnt how UPI actually works from the inside — not as a user but as someone who understood the plumbing: how merchant QR codes are reconciled, how settlement cycles function, where the infrastructure breaks. That knowledge is not available in any classroom; it is earned by being inside a fintech company during India's UPI inflection point.

His move to the **National Health Authority** during COVID was the pivot that created CotoPay. Working on CoWIN and the e-RUPI voucher framework, Aviral observed something specific: programmable digital vouchers could be issued to beneficiaries, locked to specific purposes, and tracked in real-time — without the recipient needing a new app. The same infrastructure, he realised, could solve every Indian business owner's field expense nightmare: how do you give your driver ₹2,000 for fuel and know it was actually spent on fuel?

**Vidit Sidana (Co-Founder & CBO)**
**Education:** SRCC, Delhi University → **IIM Ahmedabad** (MBA)
**Career:** 7 years consumer FMCG → **ITC** (Brand Manager, Sunfeast — ₹7,000 crore portfolio) → CotoPay

Vidit and Aviral are school friends from Dehradun — a friendship predating the business by decades. His SRCC undergraduate and IIM Ahmedabad MBA placed him at India's business education apex. Seven years at ITC managing the Sunfeast biscuits portfolio at ₹7,000 crore gave him something no tech founder typically has: deep understanding of how large Indian businesses track and audit distribution spending at scale. He has lived, at enterprise level, exactly the problem CotoPay solves. When Aviral explained the e-RUPI concept, Vidit validated the pain across industries — and quit his stable corporate career to build CotoPay's business strategy.

**Uzair Syed Ahmed (Co-Founder & CTO)**
**Background:** 20+ years software development → **Original Aadhaar team** → **Designed CoWIN architecture** (PMO invitation)

Uzair is the episode's quietly extraordinary figure. He built **Aadhaar** — the digital identity system for 1.3 billion Indians. When COVID demanded a national vaccine platform at impossible speed, the **Prime Minister's Office** invited him to design **CoWIN's architecture** — a system that managed 2.2 billion doses for 1 billion people in 18 months. When the Sharks heard this, Anupam said: *"Aadhaar… CoWIN… Solid assets. These are among the biggest systems for India."* Aman then delivered the season's most celebrated line: *"Aap teen log itne underachiever ho, kabhi bura nahi lagta?"* (You three are such underachievers — doesn't it bother you?) The room burst into laughter because the joke was true: three founders who built infrastructure touching every Indian citizen were standing in the Tank asking for ₹50 lakh.

**What their collective background explains:** CotoPay is not founded on market research. It is built by people who designed the exact infrastructure it runs on. Aviral understands e-RUPI from having built it for the government. Uzair knows how to architect billion-user systems. Vidit understands enterprise expense control from 7 years managing it at ₹7,000 crore scale. The product is the direct commercial application of accumulated institutional knowledge to a problem the founders had personally witnessed.

---

### LOCAL ALL NATURAL SODA — Zomawii Khiangte & Felipe Rodriguez

**Zomawii Khiangte** grew up in Mizoram and built a career in the United States before making a deliberate choice most people in her position don't: she returned home. In 2016, she came back to Mizoram — not for a visit but permanently — to build something rooted in the place she came from. Her conviction: the Northeast of India, with its extraordinary agricultural diversity and rich fruit varieties, has commercial potential the mainstream market has consistently ignored.

**Felipe Rodriguez**, her husband, brings a cross-cultural perspective that bridges international consumer markets with deep local rootedness. Their first venture together was **Local Beer** — a craft brewery using Northeast ingredients, shut down when Mizoram's regulatory environment shifted against alcohol production. Instead of leaving, they pivoted: same philosophy (local ingredients, artisanal craft, Northeast farmers), different product (natural soda). Under **Tui Bon Natural Private Limited** (Tui Bon = "river" in Mizo), Local All Natural Soda was born in 2021.

**What their background explains:** Zomawii's return from the US and commitment to Mizoram despite regulatory setbacks and geographic isolation reflects a specific founder type — mission-driven before commercially driven, community-rooted before investor-ready. This produces authentic products with genuine origin stories. It also produces a natural friction with investors whose growth mandates require fast, aggressive national distribution from a founder willing to prioritise scale over rootedness. Aman's "investor-founder fit" observation was not an insult — it was an accurate compatibility assessment.

---

### MYWONDER — Chandramouli Kasinathan & Venkat Potluri

**Chandramouli Kasinathan (Co-Founder, Technology)**
**Career:** Amazon → redBus → Tesco → JDA Software → Co-founded **Sellerworx** (acquired by **Capillary Technologies**, 2016) → Director of Engineering at Capillary → VP Technical Product Management at DevFactory → MyWonder

Chandramouli is a repeat entrepreneur with an acquisition already behind him. He co-founded **Sellerworx** in 2014 — an omnichannel e-commerce management SaaS backed by **Infosys co-founders Kris Gopalakrishnan and S D Shibulal**, as well as Axilor Ventures, and selected for **SAP's Startup Studio**. Capillary Technologies acquired Sellerworx in 2016. His subsequent VP role at DevFactory gave him the AI systems architecture depth to personally re-engineer MyWonder's conversational AI pipeline for real-time voice interaction on low-cost hardware — without heavy cloud processing. He describes the achievement: *"We re-engineered the entire AI pipeline to make real-time voice conversations possible without relying on heavy cloud processing."* This is genuinely difficult; most AI voice products require latency that makes child interaction frustrating. Chandramouli solved it from first principles.

**Venkat Potluri (Co-Founder & CEO)**
**Education:** **IIM Bangalore** (MBA)
**Career:** Consumer and GTM roles → Co-founded Sellerworx (confirmed by Capillary acquisition reporting and Tracxn data) → MyWonder

Venkat is the commercial mind — IIM Bangalore and Sellerworx co-founding experience give him both theoretical framework and practical scar tissue of building a B2B product from zero to acquisition. His founding motivation is directly personal: *"This is one of the most exciting opportunities of our time — to build a product that's as captivating as digital entertainment, but designed for healthier, more imaginative childhoods."*

Both founders are parents. MyWonder was conceived, as Chandramouli describes, *"in the living rooms of two parents grappling with a universal modern dilemma: how to keep their young children engaged and learning without handing them a screen."* That parental immersion earned them **Antler India's** pre-seed ($300K) and selection for **Google's AI for Startups programme** — institutional validation that the Shark Tank room acknowledged but couldn't translate into a deal at ₹80 crore.

**What their background explains:** Two founders with a prior acquisition, IIM Bangalore pedigree, deep AI engineering (Chandramouli personally rebuilt the pipeline), and Antler backing — among the strongest founding credentials in Season 5. The Sharks' no-deal was never about founder quality. It was about valuation (₹80 crore pre-commercial-revenue) and the defensibility question that credentials alone cannot answer: can a physical device compete with Alexa as AI becomes ambient?

---

### WHAT ALL THREE FOUNDING STORIES SHARE

Episode 27's founders share one defining characteristic: **all three teams built from infrastructure they personally understood at a deeper level than almost anyone else.** Aviral helped build e-RUPI. Uzair designed CoWIN. Vidit managed ₹7,000 crore of enterprise field spending. Zomawii knew Mizoram's farmers and fruits from childhood and community. Chandramouli rebuilt AI pipelines the way engineers who've worked at Amazon, redBus, and Capillary do — from first principles, not tutorials.

This is earned expertise — knowledge that comes from being inside a problem, not observing it. It is the foundation of authentic businesses, and it is visible in all three pitches. What differs is how completely each team translated that expertise into a business architecture investors can fund: CotoPay completely, with institutional credibility replacing traction. MyWonder technically but not yet commercially at the right price. Local All Natural Soda authentically, but on a timeline only patient, mission-aligned capital can support.
